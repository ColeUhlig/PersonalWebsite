The Farmerdrop money system

Farmerdrop customers buy from local farms and collect their order from a neighbour's porch, the drop host. The card is charged days before the produce is picked, and the money has to reach three different people as the crate changes hands. This is how I built that on Postgres and Stripe Connect: the SQL that computes every cent, the ledger rows each step writes, and what holds when requests race or the server dies.

Charge model
Stripe Connect Express, separate charges and transfers. The platform charges the card; farmers and hosts are paid later by transfer.
Money math
compute_order_financials, compute_refund and compute_reimbursement, all SQL
Ledger
One signed amount per row across 8 account types, including an escrow account called holding
Exactly-once
Partial unique indexes, row locks, compare-and-set updates, an idempotency-keyed outbox
Recovery
A five-minute job with eight passes: unconfirmed payments, unreported tax, failed refunds and tax reversals, Stripe's fees, unanswered claims, abandoned orders

2compute_order_financials

compute_order_financials takes the goods, the tax and four rates, and returns every party's share. It is LANGUAGE sql IMMUTABLE and calls nothing but round. Each leg is computed and rounded once, directly from the inputs; the payouts are differences of rounded legs, so they are exact cents. The platform's total is not computed at all. It is whatever remains after the farmer, the host and the tax, so the parts always sum to the order total and the platform absorbs every rounding remainder.

The table runs a BigInt port of the function in your browser, with Postgres's half-away-from-zero rounding. Edit any input. Hover a row to find its line in the SQL.

migrations/0018_fees_taxes.sql:102 reflowed for reading; identifiers and expressions unchanged
CREATE FUNCTION compute_order_financials(
  subtotal numeric, tax numeric, card_pct numeric,
  transfer_pct numeric, platform_pct numeric, drophost_pct numeric)
RETURNS TABLE (order_total numeric, farmer_gross numeric, farmer_card numeric,
  farmer_transfer numeric, farmer_payout numeric, drophost_gross numeric,
  drophost_card numeric, drophost_transfer numeric, drophost_payout numeric,
  platform_card_pct numeric, platform_total numeric)
LANGUAGE sql IMMUTABLE AS $$
SELECT g.ot,
       g.fg, g.fc, g.ft, g.fg - g.fc - g.ft,   -- farmer_payout
       g.dg, g.dc, g.dt, g.dg - g.dc - g.dt,   -- drophost_payout
       g.pcp,
       g.ot - tax - (g.fg - g.fc - g.ft) - (g.dg - g.dc - g.dt)   -- platform_total
FROM (SELECT subtotal + tax                                                    AS ot,
             round((1 - platform_pct - drophost_pct) * subtotal, 2)            AS fg,
             round(card_pct * (1 - platform_pct - drophost_pct) * (subtotal + tax), 2) AS fc,
             round(transfer_pct * (1 - platform_pct - drophost_pct) * subtotal, 2)     AS ft,
             round(drophost_pct * subtotal, 2)                                 AS dg,
             round(card_pct * drophost_pct * (subtotal + tax), 2)              AS dc,
             round(transfer_pct * drophost_pct * subtotal, 2)                  AS dt,
             round(card_pct * platform_pct * (subtotal + tax), 2)              AS pcp) g
$$;

3Card and transfer fees

The customer never sees a card fee. Stripe takes 2.9% of the whole $10.00 plus 30¢, and the cost is split by rule. The flat 30¢ comes out of the finance fee, and the remaining 25¢ of that fee is platform margin from the moment of payment. The 2.9% is charged to each payee in proportion to their share of goods and tax, inside the SQL above (fc, dc), and the platform covers the rest out of its pot.

Stripe also charges for every Connect payout. The function withholds 0.25% of each payee's goods share (ft, dt) and leaves it in holding; at payout it moves to the platform, which pays Stripe's real payout bill. Its estimate is booked as pending rows when each transfer goes out and trued up against Stripe's month-end bill.

4fee_schedules and frozen rates

Fees live in fee_schedules, one row per rate change, each with an effective_from. A new rate is a new row; the admin API refuses to edit a schedule once it is in force. When an order is placed, the schedule in force at that instant is copied onto the order's own rate columns inside the placement transaction.

Per-party dollar amounts are never stored on the order. The order_financials view derives them on every read by running the split over the order's frozen rates, so raising the platform's share next month cannot change what an existing order owes anyone, and any historical order's split can be recomputed from its own row.

The append-only rule for schedules is enforced by the application, not by a trigger, and an order stores the rates themselves rather than a schedule id.

5ledger_entries

Each row in ledger_entries belongs to an account, the pair (party_type, party_id), and carries one signed amount: positive means that account gained money. A balance is SUM(amount) WHERE status = 'confirmed'. entry_type records why a row exists and never changes a balance.

The books balance as an accounting equation rather than a zero sum: cash at Stripe equals holding plus everything owed to the farmer, the host, the tax authority and the platform. The test suite checks that equation after every phase of every money flow.

Step through the order below. Holding fills at payment and drains to zero as the crate moves: the farmer and platform are recognised when the drop host receives the crate, the host and the tax when the customer picks up, and the transfer reserve at payout.

6Exactly-once guarantees

Four different things can report the same payment: the browser, Stripe's webhook, the native app's redirect and the five-minute job. Stripe retries, admins double-click and processes die between a Stripe call and the database write. Every money step has to happen once. The places where a duplicate would cost money are backed by Postgres itself; the rest are one-time transitions done under row locks or compare-and-set updates inside a transaction.

Enforced by Postgres

8July 2026 audit

The fees, taxes and escrow ledger merged as one pull request: 109 commits, 185 files, +30,656 and −8,634 lines. A July 2026 audit of the money path, run from a red test baseline and adversarial reviews, found and fixed 26 defects. Five of them shaped the design above.

  1. The main payment path booked nothing

    The desktop success page called mark-paid, which set the order to paid without writing a ledger row. The webhook then skipped the order because it was already paid. 760 tests passed, because every test put money in through the webhook. The fix made mark-paid verify the PaymentIntent and call the single booking function.

  2. Duplicate webhooks could double-book a payment

    The already-booked check was a read followed by a write, with no lock and no constraint. The fix is the FOR UPDATE re-check plus the partial unique index in section 6.

  3. A stable payout key wedged retries

    The first fix gave each payout one fixed key. Stripe caches the first response for 24 hours, including a transient "insufficient funds", so every retry replayed the failure. Now the key is persisted once, transfers are found by transfer_group, and the key rotates only after a definitive 4xx.

  4. Tax shown at checkout was never charged

    Stripe Tax returns line items unexpanded unless asked. The quote read the aggregate, the order committed with zero tax, and the test mock returned line items unconditionally, so CI stayed green. The fix expands and pages line items, and the mock now behaves like the API.

  5. The finance fee was booked once per week

    One PaymentIntent can cover several weekly orders but charges the finance fee once. The ledger booked it per order. The fix is a once-guard keyed on the booked "Finance fee" row rather than an in-memory flag, so it survives a crash and retry.
